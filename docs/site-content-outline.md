## 3. What We Believe (`/what-we-believe/`)

- H1: "What We Believe"
- Framing line: beliefs held "according to Holy Scripture, and in the unity of the one holy Christian and apostolic Church."
- Doctrinal statement, roughly 8 sections (should be preserved near-verbatim — this is a confessional statement, not marketing copy):
  1. **The Trinity** — one true God as Father, Son, Holy Spirit, revealed through Jesus Christ
  2. **Human Nature** — spiritually dead at birth, predisposed to sin, without inherent fear/love/trust in God
  3. **Christology** — Jesus Christ, true God eternally begotten of the Father and true man, the only Savior from sin
  4. **Salvation** — justification "solely by God's undeserved kindness... through faith in Jesus Christ," not by works
  5. **The Sacraments** — Baptism, Absolution, and Holy Communion as means through which the Holy Spirit works
  6. **The Church** — exists "wherever the Gospel of Jesus Christ is purely preached and taught"
  7. **Scripture** — "without factual error," the only source and final authority
  8. **Eschatology** — Christ will return to judge the living and the dead

## 4. What To Expect (`/what-to-expect/`)

- H1: "What To Expect"
- Intro: traditional/liturgical LCMS congregation; Divine Service with Holy Communion every Sunday morning using the Lutheran Service Book.
- Walkthrough of the liturgy, in order — good candidate for a step-by-step / timeline layout in the redesign:
  1. **Liturgy** — draws on OT roots and NT foundation; refocuses attention from self onto Jesus and His gifts
  2. **Singing** — canticles/hymns of joy in Christ's redemptive work
  3. **Confession of Sins** — congregation confesses sinfulness, trusts God's mercy
  4. **Absolution** — forgiveness declared through Christ's blood, by ordained clergy
  5. **God's Word** — three readings (OT, Epistle, Gospel), Gospel as focal point
  6. **Confession of Faith** — the ancient creeds
  7. **Preaching** — Scripture applied, calling to repentance and trust in Jesus
  8. **Law & Gospel** — Law reveals sin; Gospel announces forgiveness through Christ
  9. **Offering**
  10. **Prayer** — for the church, nation, and the suffering
  11. **The Lord's Supper** — "Jesus has given to His people the special gift of His very body to eat and His very blood to drink for the forgiveness of our sins"
  12. **Blessing** — the benediction

## 12. Sacred Art (`/sacred-art/`)

- H1: "Sacred Art"
- Intro: ten paintings by Professor William Bukowski (Bethany Lutheran College) depicting Christ's life per the liturgical calendar. (Nine described in detail below — confirm the 10th with client, likely missed in original scrape.)

1. **The Nativity of Our Lord** — Mary, Joseph, baby Jesus in the manger; ox and donkey nod to Isaiah 1:3; rod/staff/lamb imagery ties Jesus to His role as shepherd and sacrificial lamb.
2. **The Boy Jesus in the Temple** — young Jesus teaching among the scholars; "He increased in wisdom and in stature and in favor with God"; Mary and Joseph find Him in dialogue with teachers.
3. **The Baptism of Our Lord** — John baptizes Jesus in the Jordan; heavens open, Spirit descends as a dove, Father's declaration; hangs in the chancel near the baptismal font.
4. **The Calling of the First Disciples** — fishermen at the Sea of Galilee called to apostolic ministry; the miraculous catch; Peter, Andrew, James, and John follow Christ.
5. **The Transfiguration of Our Lord** — Jesus radiant, flanked by Moses and Elijah, witnessed by Peter, James, John; "This is My beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased."
6. **The Lord's Supper** — first commissioned painting in the collection; hangs near the altar; commemorates the institution of Communion (1 Corinthians 11).
7. **The Crucifixion of Our Lord** — Jesus's final moments and the varied responses of witnesses; sacrificial death "for the sins of the whole world."
8. **The Resurrection of Our Lord** — risen Christ with lilies/flowers symbolizing new creation; ties Christ's resurrection to believers' future resurrection and eternal life.
9. **The Ascension of Our Lord** — Jesus ascends with hands raised in blessing (Luke's account); emphasizes joy and Christ's ongoing presence with believers in worship.
